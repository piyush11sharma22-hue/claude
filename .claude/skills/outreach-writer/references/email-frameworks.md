# Copywriting Frameworks for Cold Outreach

Pick one framework per message. Don't mix. Keep the total short — these structures work at 50–125 words for email and 2–4 sentences for DMs.

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve
Best when the prospect has a clear, known pain their role owns.

- **Problem**: name the specific pain, framed around their role/company signal.
- **Agitate**: one line on the cost of leaving it unsolved (time, money, risk).
- **Solve**: your offer as the fix, with one proof point. End with a soft CTA.

> Saw {Company} is hiring 5 backend engineers this quarter. Onboarding usually
> means weeks of tribal-knowledge Q&A before anyone ships. We cut that to days
> for {Similar Co} by auto-documenting their codebase. Worth a 15-min look?

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge
Best when you're selling a transformation or a "new way."

- **Before**: their current painful state.
- **After**: the better world once it's solved.
- **Bridge**: your product is how they get there.

> Right now your reps probably spend half their day researching accounts
> instead of selling. Imagine they walked into every call already briefed.
> That's the gap {Product} closes — it builds the brief automatically. Curious?

## AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
Best for a fuller pitch when you've earned a bit more length.

- **Attention**: a hook line about them.
- **Interest**: why it's relevant to them specifically.
- **Desire**: the outcome + proof.
- **Action**: one clear CTA.

## Question-Led
Lowest friction — optimized purely for a reply, not a pitch.

Open with a genuine, specific question tied to their situation. No pitch in touch 1.

> Quick question — how is {Company} handling {specific workflow} now that
> you've moved to {signal, e.g. "a remote-first setup"}? Asking because we
> just solved this for a few teams your size.

## Referral / Mutual Context
Highest reply rate. Use when you share a person, community, alumni network,
investor, or event.

> {Mutual name} mentioned you're the person thinking about {topic} at {Company}.
> We're helping {similar companies} with exactly that — happy to share what's
> working if useful.

---

## Subject Line Patterns (email)
Keep under ~45 characters. The subject sells the open, not the product.

- **The hook**: `{Company}'s 5 new backend hires`
- **The question**: `{workflow} at {Company}?`
- **Curiosity**: `an idea for {Company}`
- **Mutual**: `{Mutual name} suggested I reach out`
- **Specific + short**: `cutting onboarding from weeks to days`

Avoid: ALL CAPS, "Re:" fakery, emoji spam, "Quick question" (overused),
anything that reads as a mass blast.

## The Reply-Rate Checklist
Before sending, every message should pass all of these:

- [ ] First line is about *them*, and couldn't have been sent to anyone else
- [ ] Exactly one CTA, and it takes < 5 seconds to say yes to
- [ ] Under the word/char limit for the channel
- [ ] No "I hope this finds you well" / "I wanted to reach out" / buzzwords
- [ ] One proof point, not a feature list
- [ ] Reads out loud like a human talking
- [ ] Every claim is true (no fabricated personal facts)
